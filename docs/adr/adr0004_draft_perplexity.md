# Research prompt: unifying quantum-number labeling schemes for sulfur monoxide (SO) X³Σ⁻ across isotopologues

You are a spectroscopy research assistant. Answer every claim with a specific citation (author, year, journal, and where possible table/equation number) — every formula or constant you propose will be independently implemented and numerically tested against real transition data, so unsourced or "textbook-remembered" values are not useful.

## The problem

I am building one unified empirical energy-level network per isotopologue of the sulfur monoxide radical (SO), by combining measured rovibrational/rovibronic transitions from many independent published sources. This requires every source's transitions to be labeled with the same set of quantum numbers, because two transitions are only recognized as sharing an energy level when their quantum-number labels match *exactly*. Right now, for the ground X³Σ⁻ state, the available literature sources for a given isotopologue use two genuinely different, mutually incompatible labeling conventions, and I need to know how (or whether) they can be rigorously reconciled.

## The three specific mismatches that need resolving

1. **N-basis vs F1/F2/F3-basis are not simply offset labels of each other.** The naive assumption is that for spin S=1, F1 corresponds to N=J−1, F2 to N=J, and F3 to N=J+1 (or some fixed permutation), i.e. a pure Hund's case (b) label swap. This assumption is only exactly true in the limit that the rotational spacing B·N(N+1) is much larger than the spin-rotation (γ) and spin-spin (λ) coupling terms — i.e., far from J=0 in the true case-(b) limit. Whether this limit actually holds well enough for SO's X³Σ⁻ state across the J-range these sources actually measured (which includes low J, where deviation from pure case (b) is generally largest for this kind of intermediate-coupling triplet state), for *all four* isotopologues individually (not just the best-studied ³²S¹⁶O), is the central open question.

2. **e/f parity is resolved in Convention B, structurally absent in Convention A.** Every Convention-A (N-labeled) source reports a single, unresolved rotational line per (v, N, J) — there is no indication in any of these papers of whether that measured frequency corresponds to one parity component, the other, or an unresolved blend of both. Convention-B sources resolve e and f as separate, individually measured lines. If the splitting between e and f components is not negligible at the precision level of the microwave measurements, then an N-labeled line is not simply "missing" the parity label — it may be an intensity-weighted or otherwise blended average of two physically distinct energy levels, which is a different (harder) problem than a pure quantum-number relabeling.

3. **The symbol "F" is used for two physically unrelated quantities across these sources, which is a bookkeeping trap, not a physics problem.** In Convention B, F is the electronic fine-structure sublevel index (F1/F2/F3) described above. Separately, the Sanz, McCarthy & Thaddeus (2003) paper's own table also has a column labeled F, but there it means the *nuclear hyperfine* quantum number arising from the ³³S nucleus's spin (I=3/2) — a completely different quantum number that only exists for the ³³S¹⁶O isotopologue and is entirely absent physically (not just unmeasured) for ³²S¹⁶O, ³⁴S¹⁶O, and ³⁶S¹⁶O, whose relevant nuclei (³²S, ³⁴S, ³⁶S, ¹⁶O) all have zero nuclear spin. Any proposed unification scheme must not conflate these two F's.
